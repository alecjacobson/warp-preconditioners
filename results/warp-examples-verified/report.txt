Verified time-to-accuracy comparison (2026-10-06)
==============================================

This follow-up resolves the dagger-marked entries in ../warp-examples/report.txt.
All nine original systems and their stopping targets are retained unchanged.
The matrix snapshots are checked against the original SHA-256 digests.

Why the original entries needed qualification
--------------------------------------------
Most original solvers stopped using a recursively updated float32 residual.
Recomputing b-Ax exposed residual drift and solution-storage roundoff. Some
requested targets (such as convection-diffusion at 1e-12) are below what
float32 solution storage can reliably attain. Magnetostatics instead hit
its original 250-iteration cap without convergence.

Common verified solve procedure
-------------------------------
- Keep the original float32 matrix, preconditioner dtype, and CG/CR method.
- Accumulate the solution and recompute b-Ax in float64, using Warp on CUDA.
- Cast each correction RHS to float32; solve A*delta=r from zero with the
  original preconditioner, relative tolerance 0.05 and absolute tolerance 0.
- Add delta to the float64 solution and check its true residual against the
  original absolute stopping target. Stop only on that verified condition.
- Same limits for every method: 20 correction solves, at most 1000 inner
  iterations each (20000 total). Factors are constructed once per full solve.
- The output solution is float64. This is a changed solve procedure applied
  equally to both preconditioners, not a claim about the original float32
  stopping behavior or its original output precision.

Timings include preconditioner setup, the float64 matrix copy, workspace
allocation, residual evaluations, transfers of scalar norms, all correction
solves, dtype casts, updates, and solver allocations/graph creation. Input
assembly and JIT compilation are excluded. Five trials alternate method
order on an otherwise idle NVIDIA L40; cyclic GC runs outside timings.
Construction and every correction remain in Warp; no SciPy backend.
Warp revision: d76aecb17765957fba6ca323044205f74783b4d3.
FSAI sweeps widths 4/8/16/32, otherwise default settings. Tables select the
fastest converged FSAI by median setup+solve time. This is not exhaustive tuning.

Every numerical time in this table corresponds to a verified successful
solve in all five trials. No dagger or recursively inferred convergence is used.
The small elastic-shape forward difference is not treated as a substantial win.

Darcy: its original matrix has negative interior diagonal entries; FSAI
rejects it before a solve. The successful Jacobi correction runs do not
change that applicability limitation.

Magnetostatics: none of the tested variants reaches the absolute target
9.24387. After 20000 inner iterations, the Jacobi residual is about 22406;
FSAI widths 4/8/16/32 end around 26263/27862/34157/43788 respectively.
These are budget-exhaustion results, not times to a successful solution.
The example source itself notes that numerically the RHS may not be in
the image of the curl-curl matrix (example_magnetostatics.py:243). A useful
least-squares or nullspace criterion is a separate problem specification;
these results do not prove that the original target is mathematically impossible.

Validation and reproduction
---------------------------
- Independent NumPy residual checks passed on scalar and 3x3 BSR toy systems
  for CG and CR with Jacobi and FSAI, exercising the correction procedure.
- All actual-case convergence flags equal the independently recomputed
  float64 residual comparison; every measured output is finite.
- Raw per-example JSON contains all five samples, correction/iteration
  counts and true residual histories. comparison.csv separates setup/solve.
- Original captures and their regeneration are documented in
  ../warp-examples/report.txt.
